Top: map of the Pacific. Bottom: side view along the equator. Light dots are surface water. Blue dots are cold, nutrient-rich water rising from deeper water (upwelling). The dashed line is the thermocline.

Normally, trade winds push warm surface water west. In El Niño they weaken, warm water moves east, the thermocline becomes deeper in the east, and upwelling is reduced. With fewer nutrients, phytoplankton may grow less, so ocean productivity can decrease.

Temperatures: NOAA CPC ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, 10 Sept 2026. The Super El Niño view is an illustration of a stronger event.

How can warm water far offshore end up hurting pelicans?

🦐Zooplankton
🐟Small fish

Phytoplankton are at the base of many marine food webs, so less growth can mean less food further up.

🐟AnchovetaPeruvian anchovy (Engraulis ringens) is an important food fish in the Humboldt Current and the basis of one of the world's major fisheries. In strong El Niño events its suitable water can shrink, the fish may move, and numbers and access to food can decrease.
🐦SeabirdsMany depend on anchovies and other small fish. Past El Niño events caused major problems for seabirds in Peru, including pelicans.
🐋Marine mammalsThey can change where and how they feed when prey moves.

This does not mean the whole food chain always "collapses": El Niño can reduce and change marine food webs.

Not established: that every species, including orcas and blue whales, must travel farther because of the 2026 El Niño.

Can a warming ocean help anyone?

Not everyone loses

🐠🪼🐠
🪼JellyfishSome jellyfish in the Humboldt Current region have shown higher numbers during warm El Niño periods. Change can help some species while harming others. But they do not always increase just because water is warm with fewer nutrients: the response depends on the species and local conditions.
🌴Range shiftsWarm conditions can let tropical and subtropical species move into normally cooler areas. El Niño can cause this for a short time. A similar process, tropicalization, is happening over longer periods as oceans warm due to human-caused climate change.

El Niño causes temporary changes. Long-term tropicalization is mainly linked to global warming.

Why do coral reefs turn white?

Reefs under heat stress

🪸🪸🪸🪸🪸🐠🐟

Very warm seawater causes thermal stress. Bleaching happens when corals lose the algae that give them much of their color and energy. Strong El Niño events have helped cause major bleaching events in the past.

Can the Pacific change the weather far away?

Weather at a distance

These effects are called teleconnections: El Niño can affect weather far from the Pacific Ocean.

🌧Rain and stormsRainfall, storms, and flood risk can change in different regions
🌡TemperatureTemperatures can change in different regions
🏜AmazonDrought risk can increase in some parts of the Amazon
🐟SalmonIn past events, parts of the northeastern Pacific became warmer, with changes in plankton and food for young salmon. The response is not the same for every population

These are changes in probability, not guarantees. El Niño can make some patterns more likely, but it does not control the weather everywhere, and effects differ by region and season.

Most careful conclusion: not that climate change "caused" the 2026 El Niño, but that continued warming can increase background heat and make some effects of extreme climate events more serious.
